# Overnight playtest, 2026-11-28

An unattended playtest: the bot levels against each other, Sonnet playing whole games against the bots, and balance batches over player counts, nations, seats, strategies, cards and maps. Rules as in `rules/` on this date (after DD-147 and DD-148). Rule findings are proposals: nothing here changes `rules/`. What was changed is in the bots and the lab only (section 6).

## Summary

- **Levels are in order: Easy < Normal < Hard < Expert.** Expert played no better than Hard, so Hard now searches 3 candidates with 30 samples instead of 5 with 60. Expert then beats it at 2 and 4 players, by about 2 points (section 3). Against Sonnet the order holds: Sonnet's margin falls from +22 against Normal bots to +4 against Hard ones on the same seat.
- **Expert is not yet at a strong human's level.** Sonnet beat the bot's result in its seat in 16 of 18 games (14 of 16 against the Expert), by about 11 points of margin a game, and called the Expert bots casual humans. What is missing: gold and tax planning, the loyalist seat, early expansion in 2 players, the bell race.
- **Fixed tonight** (bots only, section 6): war cards now defend the last colony that can hold (a destroyed nation scores 0, DD-147), follow the whole hand's plan, and take empty loyalist colonies; search no longer passes with cards in hand. In the deterministic replays the Expert's own seat improved on 3 of 4 seeds. Two weight tunes beat the bots but made Sonnet's games easier, so both were withdrawn.
- **Balance** (section 4): the loyalist wins far too often, 43% at 3 players and 28% at 4 (target about 15%). The loyalist colony score drives it (4 🏅 instead of 6 gives 15% at 4 players, on target, P-1). Taxes are dead because nobody has gold: lowering the track barely helps (P-3). Franklin's takers win 2.3× their share, but halving his bonus does not change that: the bell leader takes him. Seats, early endings and colony survival meet their targets. 4-card nations sit 9 points below 5-card ones, as intended; within them the stars order nothing reliably.
- **Map**: room for 3 colonies each in 98–100% of maps up to 4 players, 84% at 5. Half the settlements raid from the start.
- **Strategies**: a tall bell rush wins between bots but lost against them in Sonnet's hands; becoming the loyalist on purpose hinges on an unstated tie-break (P-2). Wide plus a bell engine plus concentrated defence won 16 of 18 games.

## 1. Sonnet against the Expert bots (wave 1, the bots as they were)

Each game was replayed with an Expert bot in Sonnet's seat (`seat.ts result`), so each game compares Sonnet with the Expert on the same seed. Margin is the seat's score minus the others' mean.

| Game | Players, seat, nation | Sonnet | Expert in the same seat |
|---|---|---|---|
| pt1 `night-a` | 4, seat 0, French | 16, margin −5.7, lost | 16 as loyalist, margin +7.7, won |
| pt2 `night-b` | 4, seat 2, Curonian | 31, margin +9.0, won | margin +5.7, won |
| pt3 `night-c` | 2, seat 0, Spanish | 36 to 20, margin +16.0, won | 25 to 25, margin 0, won on gold |
| pt4 `night-d` | 3, seat 1, Curonian | 0 (destroyed), margin −11.5 | 0, margin −16.5 |

Sonnet did better than the Expert in 3 of 4 seats, by 2.7 points of margin on average (−13.4, +3.3, +16.0, +5.0). Sonnet called the Expert bots "casual humans" in every game, with one strong bell-engine bot in pt1. What it saw the bots do:

- **Pass with cards in hand**: a whole round with 5 cards, an idle ship and 13 gold (pt4, round 4); 4 cards unplayed in the last round (pt2). The lab agrees: the Expert passed with cards 4 times as often as Normal and played 2 fewer cards a game. **Fixed** (section 6).
- **War placement**: a patriot sends its cards to besiege the loyalist while its own colonies fall (pt4, and in the lab's own games, below); the loyalist stacks all its cards on one patriot colony and leaves its own at 4 defence (pt1, pt2). **Fixed in part** (section 6).
- **Bells spent on Fathers late**, ending at 0 bells and sliding into the loyalist seat by accident (pt2, pt4), and no bell engine in 2-player games (pt3: 4 bells in ten rounds).
- **One colony all game** in 2 players (pt3); taxes refused from round 2 with gold in hand (all games); food kept unspent (pt3).

## 2. The map

`mapgen.ts`, 1,000 maps a player count, everything revealed (the map does not depend on the number of players):

| | |
|---|---|
| Legal colony sites | 36.6 on average, 29 in the worst tenth |
| Colonies that fit apart (greedy packing) | 16.6 on average, 14 in the worst tenth |
| Room for 3 colonies each | 100% of maps at 2–3 players, 98% at 4, 84% at 5 |
| Native settlements placed | 6.0 (the cap), of which 3.0 start at the hostility that raids |
| Sites next to a forest | 66%; no map had fewer forest sites than players |

The map leaves room at 2–3 players, and in 98% of maps at 4; at 5 players 1 map in 6 cannot fit 15 colonies even with perfect placement (H6 is mostly answered). Half the settlements raid from their first activation, before anyone can build a stockade: the raids in rounds 2–3 that earlier notes complained of (e02, e04, s08) are built into the setup, not bad luck.

In play, the second colony is slow: at 4 players only 12% of seats have it by round 4 and 35% never found one. The bots do try: of the turns where a bot holds the 5 food and has room, 53% have a settle or found among its five best actions, and only 3% know no legal site. The cost is the logistics: food at 1 a card, then about four cards of pick-up, sailing, landing and founding.

## 3. The levels, and Sonnet against the bots (waves 2 to 4)

### The ladder, bot against bot

`ladder.ts v4`, the final bots (before the last fix): the tested level takes every seat in turn on paired seeds, the other seats the level named second. 2 players: 60 seeds, 120 games a pair; 4 players: 30 seeds, 120 games. Margin is over the others' mean; ± is the 95% interval.

| Pair | 2 players: wins (parity 50%) | margin | 4 players: wins (parity 25%) | margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Easy against Normal | 24% ±8 | −5.9 ±2.1 | 11% ±6 | −4.1 ±1.8 |
| Hard against Normal | 54% ±9 | +2.7 ±2.2 | 52% ±9 | +7.7 ±1.8 |
| Expert against Normal | 56% ±9 | +3.4 ±2.2 | 47% ±9 | +7.8 ±1.9 |
| Expert against Hard | 50% ±9 | +1.4 ±2.3 | 24% ±8 | −1.3 ±1.8 |

Easy and Normal are clearly apart, and so are Normal and Hard (strongly so at 4 players). Expert was not measurably stronger than Hard: search over 8 candidates with 150 samples settles on the same choices as 5 candidates with 60, because both stop early once the goal bot's choice cannot be beaten, and most decisions end there. The earlier ladder, with the bots as they were, gave the same picture at 2 players (Easy 23%, Hard 54%, Expert 62% against Normal).

**Fixed: Hard is now a step down.** A stronger Expert did not separate them (against Hard at 2 players, 100 games each: no early stop 57%, +1.1 ±1.8; a lower bar for leaving the goal bot's choice 53%, +0.5 ±2.4; a two-round horizon 53%, +1.7 ±2.0). So Hard now searches 3 candidates with 30 samples instead of 5 with 60 (`packages/bots/src/levels.ts`), which also halves its thinking time:

| Pair (`ladder.ts v5`, 100 games each) | 2 players: wins | margin | 4 players: wins | margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hard against Normal | 58% ±10 (parity 50%) | +4.0 ±2.6 | 34% ±9 (parity 25%) | +4.3 ±2.2 |
| Expert against Hard | 55% ±10 | +2.5 ±2.3 | 30% ±9 | +1.7 ±2.2 |

The order Easy < Normal < Hard < Expert now holds at both counts. Expert against Hard is small (pooled about +2.1 ±1.6) but above zero; against Normal, Expert's +7.8 at 4 players is well above the new Hard's +4.3.

**Sonnet's margin against the Expert rose with each tune:** +2.0 on average against the bots as they were (wave 1), +9.4 against the first tune (wave 2), +14.1 against the second (wave 3), on the same four seeds and seats. Each wave is only four games, so this is suggestive, not proven (about 1.7 standard errors from wave 1 to wave 3). But it runs against every bot-against-bot result, and the reports say why: the tuned bots trade little, so they sit at 0 gold all game. They cannot buy mercenaries, they tie for the loyalist seat on 0 bells, 0 crosses and 0 gold, and they leave the loyalist's colonies empty for Sonnet to take. **Both tunes were therefore withdrawn:** the bots keep the weights they had (section 6), and wave 4 checks the fixes that remain.

The probes, both on one seed against the same bots:

- **Becoming the loyalist on purpose does not work reliably.** Sonnet kept 0 bells, 0 crosses and 0 gold to round 10, but so did a bot, and the unstated final tie-break gave the bot the seat. Sonnet won anyway as a patriot, with three colonies, six buildings and agents taught into Captains and Dragoons. Its verdict: "fair to slightly weak as a deliberate plan, since the outcome hinges on an undefined tie" (P-2).
- **The tall bell rush lost badly.** One town hall made 4 bells a round, so 20 bells came only in round 8; by then the refused taxes had grown the Royal Army to 42, and both colonies fell. Sonnet's verdict: losing, not dominant, here. The rush needs Franklin or a politician early, and an army that stays small. Between bots, the one-colony rusher wins most (section 4); against a human who expands, it did not.

The bot-only replay in each seat is deterministic, so its change from wave 1 is the effect of the fixes alone: better on three seeds of four, −0.8 → +0.8 on average. Sonnet still did better than the Expert in three of four; s2 and pt1 are the only games of the night where the Expert's seat beat Sonnet's.

### So, is the Expert at a strong human's level?

Not yet. Over the night Sonnet beat the bot's result in its seat in 16 of 18 games (14 of 16 against the Expert), by about 11 points of margin a game, and called the Expert bots "casual humans" in every report but one (one bell-engine bot in pt1 and r1 played like a strong human). What still separates the Expert from a strong human, in the order the reports named it:

1. **Gold and taxes.** The bots refuse every tax from round 2, and either sit at 0 gold all game or hoard it unspent (26, 40, 96 gold at the war, with no mercenaries bought). A human pays the cheap early taxes for their bell and keeps gold for mercenaries.
2. **The loyalist seat.** Bots slide into it by spending bells on Fathers, or lose it on the gold tie-break, without planning either. As the loyalist they stack every card on one patriot colony and leave their own colonies empty; as patriots they left empty loyalist colonies alone (the last fix addresses this).
3. **Expansion.** In 2 players the bot keeps one colony all game; Sonnet founds three by round 5 and wins on colonies.
4. **No bell race.** Bots do not contest Franklin or the newspaper, and spend bells as they come.

These are planning faults that one-round search cannot see (section 7).

### The loyalist (O-3, O-4)

The strongest finding. The loyalist colony score (6 🏅, DD-104) sets the loyalist's win rate almost alone. Sweep `settings.score.loyalistColony`, 400 paired games a value (`out/sweep-pt-loyalist-4p-final.md`, `-3p-final.md`):

| Loyalist colony | 3 🏅 | 4 🏅 | 5 🏅 | 6 🏅 (now) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Loyalist wins, 4 players | 10% | **15%** | 21% | 30% |
| Loyalist wins, 3 players | 21% | 26% | – | 45% |

(The final bots, Normal weights; the bots before tonight's war fixes gave 12 / 19 / 25 / 37% at 4 players and 29 / 34 / – / 54% at 3.)

Destroyed nations scoring 0 (DD-147) added 8 points to the loyalist's win rate at 4 players (37% against 30% without it, bots before the war fixes): patriots who lose every colony drop out of the race. Sonnet's games say the same from the human side. In 3 players it lost every colony and scored 0 against a loyalist with 3 untouched colonies (pt4). On the same seed it later won by spending its bells on Fathers in round 9, to slide into the loyalist seat on purpose (q4). Gold decides the loyalist when several players sit at 0 bells and 0 crosses, so the player who sells well is punished (pt2, q1, q5). The rules do not say what happens when all of them tie (q4, q6, r5).

### Nations

At 4 players, 5-card nations win 26–33% and 4-card nations 15–23%: the missing card costs about 9 points of win rate, as intended (DD-19). Within the 4-card group the stars order nothing reliably: the weakest nation changed between the night's batches (Danish, 2★, 16% in one; Curonian, 4★, 15% in the last), and Swedish (3★) is consistently the best of them at 23%. Read the stars as "5 cards or 4", not as a finer order, unless a larger batch says otherwise. At 2 players all nations are inside the noise.

### Strategies

- **Bells decide.** At 4 players, Franklin's takers win 57% of their games (2.3× the average); the newspaper's and the university's 48–52%, Bolívar's 48%. Every Sonnet win ran a town hall plus Franklin (3 bells a card), and four of its reports called Franklin the strongest card. Yet halving his bonus leaves his takers' win rate where it was (58% → 56%): the bell leader takes him, rather than he makes the bell leader.
- **Tall wins among bots, not against a human.** At 2 players a seat that founded only 1 colony wins 56% (with 3 colonies: 43%). At 4 players 1 colony wins 31% and 3 colonies 23%, though 3 colonies score most (21.6 against 17.0). The one-colony bell rusher ends the game on its own terms. But Sonnet's tall rush lost (r6), while its 2–3 colonies plus a bell engine beat the bot in its seat in 16 of 18 games. Wide plus bells is the strong human line.
- **Trade barely pays.** Bots earn 17 gold a game, 9 of it from trade (4 players). The robust tuner cut the trade weight in half and won more bot games for it (section 6). Only 20% of taxes are affordable when due. H1 holds. Sonnet's big trades used a custom house: 12 crops in one sale for 48 gold (pt2), or Fugger plus a custom house for about +10 points (r3). That works because a whole sale goes at the old price (A-2), so stockpiling and selling once is the trade line that pays.
- **Taxes are dead.** Everyone refuses from round 3 at every count, bots and Sonnet alike, because nobody has the gold. The Royal Army reaches 45–66 cubes. A tax track of 0–9 gold barely changes it (P-3). Refused taxes make 30 of the Royal Army's 52 cubes at 4 players (31 of 42 at 2). The largest events are the Stamp Act (4.5 cubes a game at 4 players), El Dorado Rumors (3.6), Mutiny (2.1) and the Boston Tea Party (1.8).
- **Personalities.** Rebel (41%) and balanced (38%) lead; trader and missionary (15%) and defender (12%) trail. Crosses stay weak: the chapel is built by 32% of seats, the church never.

### Cards that are never worth it

At 4 players these are built by 2% of seats or fewer: church, factory, shipyard. Hired by 1% or fewer: slave, priest, preacher, scout, corsair, recruiter. The Fathers taken least are Las Casas and Pizarro (1–2%).

### Two weight tunes, both withdrawn

The bots' weights (BASE, which every level and character builds on) were last tuned on 25 November, before the changes to the war, scoring and bells. Two self-play tunes were tried (`tune-level.ts`, 12 generations of 12 candidates, 288 games each):

1. **Against the Normal bot only, on win share.** It beat the old bots clearly: 63–65% at 2 players, 31% at 4, 24–27% at 5. But tables of tuned bots played a worse game. Colonies a player fell from 2.0 to 1.5, patriots destroyed rose from 23% to 38% at 4 players, and solo wins fell from 73% to 48%: it had learned to exploit the old bot, not to play well.
2. **Robust (ROBUST=1).** Half the other seats played the candidate's own generation, solo games counted, and keeping a colony and the score counted beside winning; the bell weight was put back to 1 after the tune halved it. Between bots it was better everywhere: even at 2 players, +1.1 to +1.8 points at 3–5, its own nation destroyed half as often. In self-play it founded more colonies (2.23 against 1.98 at 4 players) and lost fewer (14% against 19%). It cut trade to half (the tuner found selling not worth a card, section 4) and leaned hard against the loyalist seat.

Sonnet beat both more easily than the bots they replaced (section 3), so neither was kept. The weights are in `out/playtest/tuned-night.json` and `tuned-robust.json` for a later look. The lesson for tuning: self-play fitness is not enough; a tune needs a check against human play (or a human-like opponent) before it ships.

## 7. Next steps

- **Bots** (section 3): the Expert's gaps with a strong human are plans over several rounds: keeping gold for mercenaries and cheap taxes, choosing the loyalist seat on purpose (or avoiding it), a second and third colony early in 2 players, the bell race. One-round search cannot see them. A longer search horizon does not help: Expert searching two rounds ahead against Hard won 53%, margin +1.7 ±2.0 (120 games at 2 players), no better than Expert now. What is left to try is a plan layer in the goal bot: a target round for the war, a gold reserve for it, a decision on the loyalist seat by mid-game. Any new weights need a check against Sonnet games before they ship: both of tonight's tunes beat the bots and lost ground to the human.
- **Expansion is not a weight.** Sonnet founds three colonies by round 5 in 2 players; the bots found their second in round 6 on average. But giving the Normal bot more weight on colonies loses to the bots as they are: colony 1.6 wins 42% at 2 players (parity 50%), colony 2.2 with food 1.6 wins 36%, and both are even at 4 players (150 seeds each, every seat in turn). Wide play pays only together with the bell engine and defence that Sonnet builds around it, which is the plan layer's job.
- **Where Sonnet's points come from** (wave 4 replayed round by round against the Expert in the same seat, `cmp.ts` on the frozen copy). In s3 (2 players) Sonnet had 19 gold in round 2 against the bot's 0, paid the early taxes, and the Royal Army stood at 37 cubes in round 9 against 45; it held all three colonies (18 🏅 against 12). In s1 (4 players) it held three colonies (18 🏅) while the bot rushed bells on one (6 🏅 and 10 for bells). Early gold, a smaller army and colonies kept: one plan, not three weights. More weight on trade or gold alone loses to the bots too: trade 2 wins 46% at 2 players and 20% at 4 (parity 50 / 25%), gold 0.25 wins 43% and 18% (200 seeds each, Normal level). Sonnet's opening was a colony on a silver mountain, sold in round 2; valuing a silver site by its income until the war leaves the bot where it was (margin −0.03 ±0.33 at 2 players, +0.26 ±0.48 at 3, +0.04 ±0.35 at 4, 300 seeds each), so it was not kept.
- **Expert over Hard is a small step** (about 2 points) after lightening Hard. Making Expert itself stronger by search settings did not work (no early stop, a lower bar for leaving the goal bot, a longer horizon; a 400-sample run was stopped unfinished); the plan layer above is the next lever.
